# Cut a film

**Cut** is the film's own step — the **Cut** tab, after Day and Story (`/cut`, on a phone
`/m/cut`). Day's _Ready to cut_ section and Story's **Cut film →** open it with their shots
picked.

The monitor and the timeline sit together, with one panel beside them — **Shots**, **Options**,
**Transcript**, **Subtitles**, **Continuity** (with how many are flagged), **Notes** (review
comments, with how many are open) and, when a file is gone, **Missing** —
so watching, arranging and setting up the film happen on one screen (on a narrow screen the panel
comes under the timeline). **Your films**, **Versions** and **Package** are tabs below.

## Before it cuts

**Before cutting**, in Options, is a live checklist — ✓ fine, ! worth a look, ✗ fix it first:
the shots, missing media, subtitles (when on), whether there is anything to hear after mutes, the
picture size, whether the server can encode, and its free disk space for the film. It also says
what is left for the cut itself.

When you cut, every shot is checked first (_Checking the shots…_): missing, black, a talking clip
with no sound, or a still that missed its pose or face. Then choose **Retry them first**,
**Leave them out** or **Cut anyway**. After a server encode, **The film as cut** reads the
finished file back — its size, length, frame rate, sound and loudness — against the profile.

## Shots

Each shot is a card with its still (or the clip's first frame — hover to play it), the beat and
what is said. **Drag** a card by its ⠿ grip to move it (or ↑ ↓), untick to leave it out, edit
its caption, hold a still longer, **frame** a still (waist up, chest up), **trim** a clip, pick
the **poster**, set a still's **move** (push in, pull out, hold still) and a clip's
**sound** (as recorded, louder, quieter, muted). **colour** opens the shot's own correction —
exposure, contrast, saturation and warmth — under the film's look; the monitor shows it as you
drag, the film is rendered with the same numbers, and **As shot** clears it (double-click a
slider to reset just that one). **Copy** a shot's colour and **Paste** it on another, or
**Paste to all shots**; **Match the others to this shot** makes _Match shots_ move every other
shot toward that one instead of the film's middle (it stays as shot). A clip's **move** is a slow
**push in** or **pull back** over it (about 15%, the frame cropped).

## Editing keys

| Key           | Does                                                                  |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Space · K / L | Pause / play                                                          |
| J             | Back a shot                                                           |
| ← → · , .     | Previous / next shot · one frame back / on                            |
| S             | Split the clip at the playhead                                        |
| I / O         | Trim the clip's in / out point to the playhead                        |
| F             | Freeze frame — the clip's frame held 2 s after it                     |
| D             | Duplicate · Alt+← → move the shot · Delete leave it out · Ctrl+Z undo |
| M · [ ]       | Drop a marker at the playhead · jump to the marker (or review comment) before / after |
| Shift+← →     | A second back / on · Home / End: the start / the end                  |
| + − · \       | Zoom the timeline in / out · fit the cut to it                        |
| Shift-click   | Pick several shots: Delete (or **Leave them out**) leaves them all out · Escape clears |

Dragging a still's hold edge or a clip's end **snaps** to the playhead, your markers, the music's
beats and the edges of the shots before it (a line shows where); hold **Alt** to drag freely.
Moving the pointer over the shots **skims**: the monitor shows that moment and the playhead stays
put. A clip block shows frames along it, and the clip-sound and narration lanes show their
waveforms.

The monitor's time is timecode — hours:minutes:seconds:frames at 30 frames a second, the film's
rate — so **,** and **.** step one frame of it. Beside it, a **level meter** shows the sound as it
plays: left and right loudness bars, a peak mark that holds, the loudest peak in dB, and a light
that stays red once the sound clips (click it to clear). Keep peaks under −1 dB.

**Frame** under the monitor saves the frame at the playhead as a PNG — at its picture's own size,
in the film's shape, graded as you see it, without text over it.

**Scopes** under the monitor shows a waveform (each column's brightness, black at the bottom,
white at the top) and a vectorscope (hue and saturation, with the skin-tone line) of the frame on
the monitor — look, LUT and shot colour included — and how much of it is crushed or blown.

**Speed from here…** (beside Split): a speed ramp — from the playhead the clip eases over a second
into ½×, ¼× (smooth slow motion) or 2× and plays on at it: two half-second pieces at the speeds in
between, then the rest — one Undo takes it all back. With less than 1½ s left it changes at once.

Under the monitor: **Safe** shows where Shorts, Reels and TikTok put their own buttons and
captions (vertical; a feed's bars on square; the title-safe frame on widescreen), **Loop** repeats
the shot under the playhead, the **speed** plays at 0.5×–2×, and **⛶** goes full screen.

**Make longer…** carries the clip under the playhead on in the same shot (+4, +8 or +15 s); the longer clip takes its place when it has rendered.

**New take** renders the picked shot again with a new seed; the take replaces it when ready (Remove or Ctrl+Z brings the old one back).

**Make a reel…** turns the edit into a 15 / 30 / 60 s vertical reel (shots spread across the film, clips trimmed to their middle, hard cuts, faces followed, subtitles on); the full edit is saved first as the _Full cut_ version.

**Make a reel…** also makes a **30 s** or **60 s trailer**: the same pick of shots across the film,
in the film's own shape, with its title card, soft crossfades and a closing card ("Coming soon",
or your end credits). Like a reel, the full cut is saved as a version first and the trailer as
_Trailer · N s_.

**Press kit** (under **Cut film**): the poster shot, the story in brief (each shot's beat), who is
in it, a page of stills and the credits, on one page to print or save as PDF and send with the film.

**Overlay…** on a shot adds a lower third (a name or place, and a smaller line) or a picture — in a corner for the whole shot, or **Full frame (a cutaway)** from and to a second of the shot: the picture covers it there while the shot's sound carries on (an insert over someone talking). A shot has one picture, corner or cutaway. **Clip from Gallery…** makes it a moving cutaway: a
clip from the Gallery plays full frame over that window (muted — the shot's sound carries on). **Add a logo** (in Options) puts a watermark over the whole film. A still's move can be **from… to…**: pick where the frame starts and ends.

**Export for an editor** downloads a .zip for Final Cut, DaVinci Resolve or Premiere: the media, an FCPXML and an EDL timeline, the music and the subtitles. After a cut, **Subtitles (.srt)** downloads the film's subtitles on their own.

**Render a ProRes master** (under **Cut film**) renders the whole film as ProRes 422 HQ (10-bit
4:2:2) with 24-bit sound in a `.mov` — for a colourist, a broadcaster or a festival copy. It is
large, downloaded and not kept on the Cast.

**End credits** (Options → Text & logo): type them one line each (a blank line is a gap) and they
roll up over black after the last shot — about 0.7 s a line, 5–30 s — with the music running on
under them. The monitor and the timeline show them too.

**Subtitle track** (Options → Text & logo): the subtitles also go into the MP4 as a track a
player's CC button turns on and off. On its own (with **Subtitles** off) the picture stays clean.
After a cut, **.vtt** downloads the subtitles as WebVTT beside **Subtitles (.srt)**.

**REVIEW timecode** (Options → Text & logo; the _Review copy_ profile turns it on): REVIEW and the
film's running timecode burned in at the top left, so reviewers can quote the exact frame — and a
copy that gets out is marked. A ProRes master never carries it.

**Render queue** (under **Cut film**): add a version per profile (**+ Review copy**, **+ Vertical
social**, …) and **+ ProRes master**; they render one after another with the cut as it is when each
starts, and download as they finish — not kept on the Cast. Keep the page open while it runs.

**Since the last cut** (under **Cut film**, after a film is made): what changed in the edit since —
shots left out, put back, moved or trimmed, new takes, colour, the look, the music, text — for
whoever reviews the next one.

**+ Audio-described** in the render queue makes an accessible version: every shot with nothing said
gets a short description of what is seen ("She pours coffee in the kitchen."), read in the other
voice to the lead's, so it is never taken for her.

**Render just a part** (under **Cut film**): set **From the playhead** and **To the playhead**, or
use **The loop**, and **Render this part** — the same mix, look and subtitles, as a quick review
copy of one scene. It is downloaded, not kept on the Cast, and has no "Previously on". It needs
the server encode (ffmpeg).

**Chapters**: tick **Chapter** on a marker (Notes → Your markers) and its note becomes a chapter
title written into the film (MP4 chapter marks; a marker without a note is "Chapter 1", …). The
film starts with an _Opening_ chapter if none is at 0, and a _Previously_ chapter covers a recap.
After the cut, **Copy the chapter list** gives the `0:00 Title` lines a video description takes.

**Generate a shot…** makes a missing shot or B-roll from words: a picture, or a moving clip (~5 s), in the film's shape, placed after the picked shot. It renders in the background and joins the film when ready; leaving the page doesn't lose it. With Phantom installed (Models: _Phantom 14B_ and its step-distill LoRA, about 16 GB), **With <Cast>** makes it a clip of her straight from her pictures — her face lock and reference — in about 1½ minutes, no picture drawn first: a shot of her that was never rendered. It has no sound worth keeping (muted, as generated clips are).

Each clip has a **speed** (0.25×–4×, slow motion is smooth), **reverse**, and **J / L cuts** in the shot list — _sound in early_ (hear it before it is seen; needs a trimmed start) and _sound runs on_ (keep hearing it over the next shot; needs a trimmed end).

## Sound

| Sound          | Where it comes from                                                     |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lines**      | Talking clips — the Cast's voice                                        |
| **Narration**  | Each slot's (or Story scene's) Narration, recorded when the film is cut |
| **Room sound** | Under talking clips, and **Add sound** on silent ones                   |
| **Music**      | Your track or **Score this film**                                       |

**Tracks**, under the timeline: **M** mutes a track — clip sound (talking clips' lines with it),
narration, music, ambience or effects — silent in the monitor and the mix preview, and left out
of the film (the status says which). **S** solos one in the monitor only; it is never saved or
encoded.

**Effects**, under the tracks: a door slam, a phone buzz. **+ Sound at 0:12** places an audio file
where the playhead is; each sound has its start (**at**, seconds into the film), its level and
its own **M**, and **×** removes it (Ctrl+Z puts it back). Clicking its name moves the playhead
there. The monitor plays them and the encode mixes them on top of everything else — they don't
duck the music. They stay at their film time: if the shots before one change length, move it.

**Make a sound** writes one from words instead of a file: type it ("a door slams shut", "rain on a
window"), pick how long (1–8 s) and **Make it** — about half a minute on LTX-2.5, then it's placed
where the playhead was, like any other effect. It makes one sound at a time; "buzzes twice" may
come out once.

**Music level points**: double-click the music lane to add a point; drag it sideways (time) or up
and down (level — 0 at the bottom, as set in the middle, twice at the top). The music follows a
line through them, on top of its level, fades and ducking — up under one moment, back down after.
A point is a slider: ↑ / ↓ change it, Delete (or Alt-click) removes it. **Clear** under _Music
start, fades & level_ removes them all. The monitor plays it (it can't go above the track's own
level there; the film can).

**Clip level points** work the same way on each clip's block in the clip-sound lane: double-click
to add a point, drag it — to dip one clip's sound under a narration and bring it back.

**Hit the loudness target** (Options → Sound, on): the finished film is measured and brought to
its profile's loudness — −14 LUFS for web and social, −23 for broadcast — with peaks kept under
−1 dBTP. One gain for the whole film (two-pass loudnorm), so quiet and loud moments keep their
balance.

**Stems too** (Options → Sound): with the film, a `.zip` of its mix's parts as 24-bit WAVs —
`dialogue` (clips' sound and narration), `music` (ducked as in the mix), `ambience`, `effects` —
each as long as the film and at its loudness, so they line up and add up to the mix, for a sound
mixer.

**Even out voices** (Options → Sound): a gentle compressor on the dialogue — clips' lines and
narration — so a quiet line and a loud one sit closer; in the film, not the monitor.

**Clean up voices** (Options → Sound): on the clips' sound and the narration, a low cut under the
voice, noise reduction and a gentle de-esser — applied in the film, not the monitor.

**Foley** (under the effects): the sounds the shots say happen — rain on the street shot, a door,
footsteps, a phone, a café's murmur, the sea — each with its moment. **Make it** makes one with
_Make a sound_ (LTX-2.5, about half a minute) and places it; **Make all** does them in turn; **Skip**
hides one. A sound already placed near that moment isn't suggested again. Places and weather go
under the whole shot, quieter.

**Read the lines on stills** (Options → Sound): a still says its line only once it is animated,
so a cut of stills is silent. With this on, each still's line is read when the film is cut — the
lead's in her own kept voice, another Cast member's in theirs (by the name the shot gives), anyone
else in a generic voice of the other sex — and the still is held for as long as the line takes.
A cut of a whole episode's kept stills becomes a talking animatic.

**Music by scene**: where the film's feeling turns — happy, then sad, then tense — a cue in that
style for each stretch (ACE-Step, about ten seconds each), placed at its start and stopped where
the next starts. A stretch longer than two minutes is several cues in the same style (a score can
be at most four minutes), so a half-hour episode has music from start to end. The cues are music,
not sounds: each fades in, plays under the score if there is one, and ducks under the lines the
way the music track does (in the render; the monitor plays them at their level). It shows when
the feeling changes, or when the film is too long for one score; for one feeling over a short
film, use **Score this film**.

**Measure loudness** on a film in **Your films** reads its loudness (LUFS), range and true peak
and says how it sits against the web's −14 LUFS or broadcast's −23 (EBU R128): what to turn up or
down, and peaks over −1 dBTP that may clip.

## Subtitles

The **Subtitles** section lists each shot with its subtitle — type one to replace the line or
narration, set **from** / **to** (seconds into the shot) to say when it shows, **Reset** to go
back, **None** for no subtitle. Overlaps, subtitles under 0.8 s or over 7 s, more than two rows and
characters a subtitle file can't carry are flagged.

**Split into cues** turns a long subtitle into timed cues — its sentences, else two halves at the
middle word — each with its own text and **from** / **to**. **Split** divides a cue again,
**Join** makes it one with the next, **One subtitle** goes back to a single subtitle over the
shot. Without cues, a subtitle is still shown phrase by phrase across its window; cues set exactly
when each phrase shows.

The monitor shows the subtitle at the playhead (**CC** hides it), and the encode burns in the
same cues at the same times.

**Subtitle look** (Options → Text & logo) sets how they are drawn: **Classic** (white on a dark
band at the bottom), **Bold** (big white words with a black outline), **Pop** (big yellow words
with an outline) or **Word by word** (a few big words at a time as they are said — the short-video
look). The outlined looks sit higher, clear of a phone's own buttons on Shorts and Reels. The .srt
always keeps whole phrases.

**Export .srt** downloads them any time. **Import .srt…** reads a subtitle file, places each
line on the shot it falls in (a shot with several keeps each as a timed cue), and shows the result
before **Use these subtitles** (one Ctrl+Z takes it back).

## Continuity and reports

**Continuity** (a tab beside the shots) lists what the checks find on the film's shots: an outfit
that jumps at the same place and time, a shot made in another look, a plate that drifted,
something carried that goes missing, and — when two shots of the same two people at the same place
put her on opposite sides of the frame — **crosses the line** (the 180° rule: their eyelines
cross). Above it:

- **Colour script** — each shot's colour as the film shows it, as long as it runs: the film's
  colour arc, and the shot that doesn't belong. Click one to pick it.
- **Continuity report** — every shot's who, wearing, where and when, what she carries and how she
  has changed, then the open findings and a **prop list** (each thing and the shots it is in): a
  page to print or save as PDF.

**Transcript** is also the dialogue pass: each line shows who says it and whether its sound is
there — **Talking clip**, **Voice-over**, or **No sound yet** (a line on a still, heard once the shot
is animated). Tick **Only the lines with no sound yet** to work through them; **Fix the line** opens
it where it is written (the Day slot's sheet, the Scene, the Story) — change it and Animate again,
and it is said in the Cast's voice, lip-synced.

**Dialogue list** (under **Cut film**) lists every line said on screen and every voice-over, shot by
shot, with who says it and whether its sound is there yet.

**Shot report** (under **Cut film**) lists what made each shot — tool, model, seed, size and steps,
LoRAs, prompt — from its Gallery record, to remake or credit it.

## Long films

A film of more than 40 shots — a half-hour episode — is rendered in pieces of about 30 shots, joined,
then given its music, ambience, effects and finish in one pass over the whole film, so a long cut
never has to hold hundreds of clips at once. It looks and sounds the same as a short one, with two
differences: shots are joined with a hard cut where one piece meets the next (crossfades inside a
piece are kept), and stems are not made at this length. A 600-shot, 20-minute test film rendered
in under two minutes on the server (stills and short clips; real clips take longer).

## Content Credentials

A film rendered on the server is signed with **Content Credentials** (C2PA, the standard behind the
"CR" label on LinkedIn, Adobe and some news sites), unless you turn **Content Credentials** off
under Format. The signed record inside the MP4 (or master .mov) says the film was edited in
Castcut from AI-made pictures and clips, names the engines its shots came from, and holds a
fingerprint of the file: change a single byte and readers report the record no longer matches.
Check one at [contentcredentials.org/verify](https://contentcredentials.org/verify) or with
`c2patool`. The status line says when a film was signed, or why it wasn't.

It's signed by this install's own certificate, made the first time it signs and kept in the data
folder (`c2pa/`). Readers show the signature as valid but the signer as **unrecognised** — it
proves what the file is and that it wasn't changed, not who you are. To sign as you, set
`CASTCUT_C2PA_CERT_FILE` (the certificate chain, PEM, yours first) and `CASTCUT_C2PA_KEY_FILE`
(its private key) to a certificate from an authority on the C2PA trust list. A film made in
the browser (no server encode), a film over 1.5 GB, and a WebM aren't signed.

## Missing media

Each shot's file is checked as the edit loads — a byte each, nothing downloaded. A shot whose file
is gone is listed under **Missing media** (it can't play as it is); one whose Gallery entry was
removed, or whose source is gone, is listed to look at. **Relink** points the shot at another
take of its source (shown inline for Scenes) or **Relink from Gallery…** — its trim kept where it
fits, **Back to the earlier take** offered, Ctrl+Z to undo. **Check again** looks once more.

## Package a cut

**Package with media** puts the cut — its edit, added shots, options and versions — and every
picture, clip and sound it plays into one `.castcut.zip`; **Package (light)** carries the
addresses only, for a machine that has the files. **Import a package…** (on any machine, even an
empty one) brings it in as a version, never over your edit or your saved versions: files already
there are reused, others are stored in that Gallery, small ones kept in the cut when the server
keeps no files. Anything it can't find shows under Missing media.

## On two devices

The edit saves as you work, and Ctrl+Z works after a reload. Saved versions are kept per version
across devices. If two devices edit the same cut, the newer edit stays and the other is kept as a
version, _Edit from another device (time)_ — neither is lost.

## Dub into another language

Day's **Dub** (beside **Animate all**) translates every line and reply with your LLM, then
makes the talking clips again with the lines said in that language — lip-synced, like English.
Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese.

- The translated lines show in each slot's **Line** — fix any wording there and Animate again.
- **As written** puts the original lines back.

!!! tip "Use a capable LLM"
An 8B model translated well in testing; a 3B one changed the meaning of some lines.

## After the cut

On the **Cast page → Film & media → Films**, under each film:

| Button                   | What it does                                                            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Re-cut**               | The same film at another shape (vertical, square, widescreen) or size   |
| **Export for an editor** | A zip with FCPXML (Resolve, Final Cut), an EDL, subtitles and the clips |
| **Share for review**     | A read-only link that expires — anyone can watch it without an account  |

!!! note
These need a film cut in **2.6 or later** (it keeps the recipe). For an older film, cut
the Day again.

## When a shot gets a new take

A shot in Cut comes from its source: a Scene's kept take, a Day slot's still or a Story beat. As long as you haven't touched it, it **follows its source**: keep another take in Scene, or redo the Day slot, and Cut shows the new one.

Once you **edit a shot** (trim, hold, framing, speed, caption, overlay…), **save a version** or **cut the film**, the shot is **pinned** to the take it was cut with. A new take in its source no longer replaces it. The shot's card says **New take available** instead:

- **Use it here**: this shot plays the new take. A clip's trim stays when it fits the new clip, ends at the clip's end when only its end ran past, and is reset when it can't apply — the status says which. **Back to the earlier take** then puts the take and its trim back — again for the take before that, up to five takes back.
- **Use for every copy**: the shot and its copies in this edit (Duplicate, Split) switch. Saved versions keep their own takes.
- **Keep this one**: it stays on the take it was cut with.

**Pin** / **Unpin** on a card does the same by hand: unpinned, the shot follows its source again. If a pinned shot's scene is deleted or its take let go, the shot keeps playing the take it was cut with and says its source is gone; unpin it to let it leave the film. Ctrl+Z undoes any of these.
